Food business permit and licence renewal tracker for India and the Philippines

A food business holds six to eight permits from different authorities on different cycles. Losing track of one closes the shop. Keep them all in one register, get escalating reminders, and plan the January renewal rush in the order the permits actually have to be done.

Is any permit expired such that we are trading unlawfully?

This answers from your register and from nothing else. A permit you have not added cannot appear here, and a date typed wrongly stays wrong. Permits from the starting lists that you have not added are shown separately under the register as gaps — a gap is a prompt to decide whether you need that permit, never a finding that you are breaking the law.

What this permit waits on

A permit that cannot be had until another is in hand. The order drives the season plan. Links the law fixes are marked as such; the rest are conventions you should check against your own office.

Saving a short code that is already on the register for the same premises updates that permit. Recording a renewal replaces the record it renews.

Renewal readiness

"Renew the business permit" is not one task — it is a barangay clearance, three inspections, a sworn declaration of gross receipts and a queue. Tick these off as you gather them. They are saved as you go.

What being late adds

This tool does not know what your permit costs — that is assessed by your own city or municipality from your own gross receipts, or set by your own state's by-law, and no national figure exists. Type in the amount you were assessed and this works out what the delay adds to it at the rates under Data & settings.

Escalating reminders

Days before expiry. The defaults are 60, 30 and 7: far enough out to start a renewal that needs an inspection, the last comfortable moment, and an emergency. They are this tool's own convention, not a requirement of any authority. Set your own.

Late payment rates

Enter a rate as a fraction: 0.25 for twenty-five per cent. The Philippine defaults are the ceilings of section 168 of the Local Government Code of 1991 (Republic Act No. 7160) — a surcharge not exceeding 25 per cent of the amount not paid on time, interest not exceeding 2 per cent a month on the unpaid amount including the surcharge, and a total interest limit of thirty-six months. Your own revenue code sets the rate that applies to you, at or below them. This tool ships no India figure at all: FSSAI's own 2025 notice says the ₹100-a-day pre-expiry late fee has been discontinued and we found no final order settling it, so the India rates start at nought and you enter what you are actually charged.

Start from a jurisdiction preset

A named starting list for a market, added to the premises selected at the top of the page. Every entry carries the authority that issues it and a note on how much the validity varies locally. Nothing is added twice and nothing you have typed is overwritten. Delete the entries that do not apply to you — the FDA licence to operate, the police eating house licence and the pollution consent apply to some food businesses and not others.

Export and import

One CSV holds the whole register. Open it in any spreadsheet and bring it back when you are done. Checklist ticks are not carried in the file; an imported permit gets its starting checklist back, unticked.

Where your data lives

Everything is in one JSON file. Back it up by copying that file. No certificate is ever uploaded or stored — each permit holds a reference to where you keep the paper, and this tool never reads it.



    
The tier question is a different tool's job

    
Which of the three FSSAI tiers an Indian food business needs — Basic Registration, State Licence or Central Licence — has its own thresholds and its own reasoning, and this tracker does not try to answer it. It watches the licence you hold. The address below is plain text so you can copy it into your own browser; this tool makes no outbound request of any kind.
